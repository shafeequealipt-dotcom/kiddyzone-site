Finding a birthday gift for a child is easy when it's your own child, and much harder when the invitation comes from a neighbour or a cousin you see twice a year. Across Malappuram, most of us have stood in a shop the evening before a party, wondering what a five-year-old actually wants.

This guide gives you a simple way to decide, without spending ages on it. Treat the age groups as a rough guide only. Always follow the age advice printed on the toy's own packaging, and remember that every child is different.

## Babies and toddlers: think about the senses

Very young children enjoy things they can hold, shake, push and look at. Bright colours, interesting textures and simple cause and effect, such as pressing something and hearing a sound, tend to go down well.

Check the packaging for the recommended age, and be careful with small parts for children who still put things in their mouths. Soft toys, simple stacking sets and push-along toys are common choices for this age.

## Ages three to five: think about pretending

Around this age, many children start to act out the world around them. They cook imaginary meals, run pretend shops and drive tiny buses along the veranda. Toys that feed this imagination often last well beyond the birthday week.

- Pretend play sets, like kitchens, tools or doctor kits
- Vehicles and simple building blocks
- Large-piece puzzles and picture books
- Art supplies, if the parents won't mind the mess

## Ages six to nine: think about challenges

School-age children often like to build something, finish something or beat someone. Construction sets, board games, card games and outdoor toys give them that feeling of having done something themselves.

If you can, ask what they're into at the moment. A child who loves cars, cricket or drawing will be far happier with a gift that fits that interest than with something more expensive that doesn't.

## Older children: ask, or let them choose

Older children have firm opinions. If you don't know them well, it's perfectly fine to ask the parents, or to go for something open-ended such as a good game or a craft kit. Outfits work well too, but check the size first, and keep the bill in case an exchange is needed.

## Three quick questions to ask the parents

A short message before the party can save you a lot of guessing:

1. What is the child really into right now?
2. Is there anything they already have too many of?
3. Is there any noise, mess or space problem we should avoid?

That third question matters more than you'd think. Parents remember who gave their child the drum kit.

## Don't forget the wrapping and the card

Children love tearing paper off a box, so a little wrapping goes a long way. Ask for the bill to be kept separately, and write your name on the card, because after a big party the parents will be trying to remember who gave what.

## Common questions

**What if I don't know the child's age exactly?**

Ask the parents, or choose something that suits a wider age range, such as a board game with simple rules or a set of art supplies. Check the age printed on the box before you buy.

**Is a toy or an outfit the better gift?**

Toys are usually the safer choice when you don't know the child's size. Clothes make a lovely gift when you can check the size with the family first.
