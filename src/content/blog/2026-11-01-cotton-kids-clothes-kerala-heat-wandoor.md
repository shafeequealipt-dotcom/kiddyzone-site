Choosing comfortable kids' clothes in Kerala comes down to one question: will this still feel good at three in the afternoon? In Wandoor, a child can spend the morning running around outside, sit through a family function at noon and fall asleep in the car on the way home, all in the same outfit. It's worth taking a minute over what you pick.

Here are the simple checks we'd suggest, whether you're shopping with us or anywhere else.

## Start with how the fabric feels

Before you look at the design, rub the fabric gently between your fingers, then press it against the inside of your wrist. That's closer to how it will feel on a child than touching it with your fingertips.

In humid weather, lighter and more breathable fabrics are usually the more comfortable choice for long hours. Heavier fabrics, stiff linings and a lot of decoration can still be lovely, but they tend to suit a short function better than a full day.

- Hold the fabric up to the light. A lighter weave usually lets more air through.
- Check the inside as well as the outside. A soft outer layer can hide a scratchy lining.
- Ask yourself how the fabric will look after a wash, especially for clothes worn every week.

## Check the fit with room to move

Children don't stand still, so test an outfit the way they'll wear it. Ask them to lift their arms, sit down and crouch. If the top rides up or the waist digs in, you'll probably hear about it later.

A little extra room is sensible, since children grow quickly and loose clothes let air move. Too much room brings its own problems though. Long hems and very wide sleeves can catch on things when a child is running or climbing stairs.

## Look closely at the small details

Comfort problems often come from the parts you notice last. Turn the garment inside out and have a look at these:

- **Neck labels and tags.** If a tag feels rough to you, it will probably bother your child too.
- **Seams.** Bulky seams at the shoulders and waist can rub in hot weather.
- **Fastenings.** Young children do better with buttons and zips that are easy to open, especially for toilet trips.
- **Decorations.** Give sequins, beads and bows a gentle tug, and check that they don't scratch on the inside.

## Match the outfit to the day

Keep it simple. Choose easy, washable pieces for everyday wear, and save the heavier outfits for occasions. For a wedding or an Eid gathering, many parents bring a change of clothes, so the child can dress up for the photos and be comfortable for the rest of the day.

During the monsoon, it helps to think about drying time as well. Thick fabrics can stay damp for a long while when the rain won't stop, so a few quick-drying pieces can make the week easier.

## Common questions

**What should I tell a shop when I'm buying for a child who isn't with me?**

Share the child's age, their usual size if you know it, and whether they are tall or small for their age. A photo of an outfit that fits them well is also a big help.

**Should I buy a size up?**

For everyday clothes, a little room usually makes sense because children grow fast. For a function next week, buy what fits well now, so the outfit looks right and stays comfortable on the day.
